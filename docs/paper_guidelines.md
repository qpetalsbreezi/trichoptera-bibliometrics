# Paper guidelines (from advisor meeting)

Principles for writing the manuscript. Use alongside `docs/paper.md` and `analysis/combined/overall_bibliometric_report.md`.

---

## Workflow

- Lock down the **analysis first**, then write.
- Order of writing: **Methods → Results → Discussion → Title & Introduction** (intro and title last).

---

## Tables vs graphs vs prose

- **Exact values → tables.**
- **Trends over time → graphs.**
- **Tables are strong** — use them generously, especially for comparative metrics.
- **Graphs only when a trend or pattern needs visual support.**
- If a point is **clear in one sentence**, **don’t make a figure** for it.
- Structure each finding as: **table or graph → then explain what it means in writing.**
- **Writing is often more important than another chart** — don’t just show data; interpret it.

---

## How to write Results

- Every subsection should answer: **what does this show, and what does it mean?**
- Start with a **strong first sentence** (the main claim).
- Let the **second sentence open with an example** (“For example…”).
- Use **plain language** — define terms so any reader can follow.
- Write **figure legends for everything** you keep.
- Legends should explain: **what was plotted, how it was calculated, what the colors/symbols mean, and the main finding.**

---

## Sample focus

- Center the paper on **taxon-focused papers**, not the full coded corpus.
- Report **what % of records were taxon-focused**.
- **RQ1 (database differences)** is secondary — may not be very interesting as a main story.

---

## Taxon order

Use this order consistently in **tables, figures, and text**:

**Culicidae → Ephemeroptera → Plecoptera → Trichoptera → Odonata**

---

## Geographic and database content

- **How databases differ** and **continental patterns** are good core content.
- Prefer **one combined figure** instead of many similar ones (e.g., don’t split continental composition across multiple graphs).
- **Histograms / grouped bars** are often easier to read than complex alternatives (e.g., heatmaps or many line panels).
- For **continental change**: use **consistent colors per continent**; **red = increase, blue = decrease** where applicable.

---

## Figure instincts

- **Opening volume figure:** convey **differences in how many articles each group/search examined**.
- **Period comparisons:** legend must state **early vs recent windows, method, and main result**.
- **Temporal trends:** prefer **arithmetic scale**; filtered views can be more informative than unfiltered ones.
- **Similar line graphs** that mostly look the same across taxa are **low value** unless one taxon clearly differs.
- **Theme figures:** label themes clearly; explain what **#1, #2, #3** mean in the legend.
- **Affiliation coverage (~95–97%):** can be **one sentence**, not a figure.
- Say **“number of authors”**, not **“team size.”**
- **International collaboration:** explain **how you measured it** in the text.

---

## Interpretation

- Don’t stop at description — address **why patterns might exist** (e.g., why Odonata falls between EPT and mosquitoes on some metrics).
- Some results belong in **Discussion**, not Results.

---

## Literature

- Supporting literature is expected.
- **AI-assisted search is fine**, but only with **carefully thought-out prompts**.

---

## Decision rule (before adding any table or figure)

1. Is this an **exact value** (table) or a **trend** (graph)?
2. Can I say it clearly in **one sentence** instead?
3. Does this visual **actually illustrate something important**, or is it redundant?
